# Why This Principle Exists

## The Problem

AI tools are becoming essential to modern software development.

However, they introduce a new type of dependency.

When AI configurations live inside repositories:

- Teams become locked into a single AI system
- Developers lose personal flexibility
- Projects become fragile when tools change
- Collaboration suffers across different AI preferences

---

## The Historical Precedent

We already separate personal tools from shared products:

| Tool Type | In Repository? | Reason |
|-----------|----------------|--------|
| IDE preferences | No | Personal choice |
| Keyboard layouts | No | Personal choice |
| Terminal settings | No | Personal choice |
| **AI assistants** | **No** | **Personal choice** |

AI assistants are development tools, not product components.

See the [Principle]({{ site.baseurl }}/principle/) for the complete separation guidelines.

---

## The Benefits

### For Individual Developers

- Use any AI tool without modifying the project
- Keep personal workflows private
- Switch between AI systems freely

### For Teams

- Support multiple developers with different AI preferences
- Eliminate conflicts over AI configuration
- Maintain a tool-agnostic repository

### For Projects

- Survive the evolution of AI tools
- Remain accessible to all developers
- Focus on product quality, not tooling

---

## The Core Insight

The repository is the **shared reality**.

AI workflows are **personal creativity**.

Different developers should bring different intelligence to the same codebase.

The project should survive the evolution of all agents.
